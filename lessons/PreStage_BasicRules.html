Step 3
発音のコツ — Pronunciation Points
A few rules that change meaning completely if missed — pay close attention to these from day one.
長音Long Vowels（ちょうおん）
Some vowels are held for twice as long. This is not decoration — it changes the word entirely.
Examples
1
おばさん ／ おばあさん
aunt / grandmother
2
ゆき ／ ゆうき
snow / courage
促音Small っ — the Glottal Stop（そくおん）
A small っ before another consonant means: hold a tiny silent beat, then release into the next sound.
Examples
1
きて ／ きって
come (te-form) / postage stamp
2
かこ ／ かっこいい
the past / cool-looking
撥音ん — The N Sound（はつおん）
ん is its own full syllable, always exactly one beat, even though it's a consonant with no vowel.
Examples
1
ほん
book (2 beats: ho-n)
2
せんせい
teacher (4 beats: se-n-se-i)
Step 4
日本語の文の仕組み — How a Japanese Sentence is Built
English word order is Subject → Verb → Object. Japanese word order is Subject → Object → Verb — and small "marker" words called particles (助詞) show each word's role, no matter where it sits in the sentence.
わたしは
Subject + topic marker は
"As for me"
→
すしを
Object + marker を
→
たべます。
Verb (always comes last)
Full sentence
1
わたしはすしをたべます。
I eat sushi. (literally: "As for me, sushi, eat.")
Because particles like は and を mark each word's job, Japanese word order is actually more flexible than English — the verb just always goes at the very end. You'll start learning particles one at a time from Stage 1, beginning with は.
